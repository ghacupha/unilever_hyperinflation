## Financial Health

**Overall Grade: C**

The grade comes from a simple rule that this model defines itself. It is not a Unilever-published or IFRS-defined scale. For each hyperinflationary subsidiary, the absolute net monetary gain/(loss) is divided by that subsidiary's total assets, and the result is mapped to a letter grade. The overall grade (C) matches Argentina's, the weaker of the two subsidiary grades.

- **Argentina: C.** Net monetary loss of €(206.0)m against total assets of €1,124.9m, an exposure ratio of (18.3)%.
- **Türkiye: A.** Net monetary gain of €11.0m against total assets of €419.8m, an exposure ratio of 2.6%.

Argentina alone pulls the overall grade down to C. Türkiye's grade reflects a small net monetary gain relative to its subsidiary assets (€11.0m on €419.8m). Argentina's net monetary loss is not small: it equals almost a fifth of the subsidiary's total assets. That loss does not come from a monetary position losing purchasing power. The model's solved Argentine balance sheet is in a net monetary *liability* position, which on the textbook heuristic should produce a gain. The loss arises because the monetary plug nets against growth in restated opening equity and operating profit. The grade therefore reflects the model's mechanics, not evidence about how Unilever actually manages the Argentine balance sheet: the subsidiary-level monetary split is an illustrative modelling choice calibrated to Unilever's disclosed aggregates, not a reported subsidiary balance sheet. At group level the impact stays contained. The combined net monetary loss of €(195.0)m is about 2.3% of group operating profit of €8,496m (an illustrative group scale, not Unilever's reported consolidated figure), which the model flags as immaterial. The Argentina exposure is still the line to watch.

These grades are for **2024**, the primary calibrated year. For that year, the model's local-currency inputs were solved to reproduce Unilever's actual disclosed figures. The 2025 roll-forward is only a directional validation check and should not be read as a snapshot of the current position.
