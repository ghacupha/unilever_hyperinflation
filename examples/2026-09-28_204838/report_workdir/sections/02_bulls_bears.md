## Bulls Say
- **The monetary hit is small next to group earnings.** The 2024 net monetary loss of about €195m is about 2.3% of group operating profit (€8,496m), well under the model's 10% materiality threshold. Colgate-Palmolive reached the same qualitative conclusion ("not expected to have a material impact") about its own highly-inflationary subsidiaries (Argentina, Türkiye and Nigeria), independently and under US GAAP.
- **Restatement barely moves reported operating profit.** Moving from plain current-rate translation (World A) to IAS 29 + IAS 21 (World C) adds about €417m of turnover, but restated costs absorb nearly all of it: COGS alone rises by about €255m. Operating profit ends only about €6m higher (€8,490m vs €8,496m). The headline profit line tells the same story under either treatment.
- **Türkiye is a small exposure.** Türkiye earns the model's top monetary-exposure grade (A), with a small net monetary *gain* of about €11m and an exposure ratio of just 2.6% on about €420m of subsidiary assets. The grade reflects a small net monetary gain relative to subsidiary assets (€10.999m on €419.82m), not hedging: the modelled subsidiary holds no protective assets. Only one of the two hyperinflationary subsidiaries is a real drag.
- **IFRS gives the less punitive result.** Under the IAS 29 treatment Unilever actually uses (World C), the net monetary loss is about €195m. The temporal-method alternative (World B) would show about €272m. World C also reports higher restated equity (about €20,785m vs €20,246m under World A) and lower leverage (debt-to-equity 1.24x vs 1.27x).
- **Consensus remains constructive.** MarketScreener's consensus across 16 analysts is Outperform (target €61.43, range €45.65–€70.24), and Stockopedia's consensus target is 5,287.14p (+13.35% vs the 25 September 2026 close of 4,664.50p per the source; about +13.19% on our calculation vs the 28 September 2026 close of 4,671.00p). The market treats Argentina and Türkiye as growth stories: management cited "high single digit growth in Argentina" and strong performance in Turkey on the Q1 2026 call.

## Bears Say
- **Argentina's modelled exposure is the weaker of the two and runs against intuition.** Argentina carries a monetary-exposure grade of C on the model's A–F scale, the weaker of the two subsidiary grades: a net monetary loss of about €206m on about €1,125m of subsidiary assets, an exposure ratio of about −18.3%. That single grade drags the group's overall grade down to C. In the model it books a loss despite sitting in a net monetary *liability* position, which should normally produce a gain. That position is a model output, solved from the balance-sheet plug given illustrative opening equity and monetary split, not a Unilever disclosure. The grade reflects the model's mechanics, not evidence about how management runs the balance sheet.
- **The 2024 tailwind reversed in 2025.** In 2024, local inflation outpaced FX depreciation, and the IAS 29 impact was positive (+€417m turnover, +€6m operating profit). In 2025, Argentina floated the peso and the lira kept sliding, and Unilever's disclosed impacts turned negative: Argentina −€54m and Türkiye −€46m operating profit, plus −€90m and −€16m turnover. Which way the effect runs depends on the macro regime, not on management.
- **The "immaterial" verdict rests on an illustrative model with known gaps.** The model's 2025 roll-forward misses the sign of total assets for both subsidiaries (Argentina +€184m modelled vs −€199m disclosed). It also overshoots Türkiye's net monetary line (−€113m modelled vs −€10m disclosed). The 2.3% materiality ratio is measured against an illustrative group scale, not Unilever's reported operating profit, so it is indicative only.
- **The market isn't looking at this line.** No public analyst commentary separates the IAS 29 net monetary gain/(loss) from ordinary FX. Currency is only discussed in aggregate: it cut H1 2026 turnover by 4.9% and underlying EPS growth by about 6 percentage points. Consensus is also less unanimous than the headline rating suggests: TipRanks' average of 5,148.89p includes 2 Sell ratings, and MarketScreener's range runs from €45.65 to €70.24. Paywalled broker notes could not be checked.
- **There is no exit in sight and no hedge.** Argentina has been hyperinflationary since July 2018. Türkiye remains classified that way on IAS 29's qualitative indicators even though headline inflation has eased toward about 31%, consistent with the IFRS Interpretations Committee's July 2025 agenda decision. Unlike BBVA, whose inflation-linked bonds offset €1,202m of its €2,118m 2023 Türkiye monetary loss, the modelled subsidiaries hold no protective assets. Restatement also inflates the asset base, trimming ROA to 18.25% under IAS 29 from 18.45% under World A.
