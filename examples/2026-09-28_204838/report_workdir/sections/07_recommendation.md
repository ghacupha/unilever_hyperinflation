## Recommendation

**Recommendation: Hold** *(on the narrow question of whether the market misprices Unilever's IAS 29 hyperinflation effect; this is not a view on the stock as a whole)*

This model does not produce an intrinsic-value target price. It has no DCF or multiples build. The only question it answers is whether the hyperinflation accounting effect is a source of earnings-quality mispricing. The answer is no, for the following reasons.

**1. The effect is immaterial at group level.** In 2024 the net monetary loss was €-195m. Modelled group operating profit was €8,496m, so the loss is about 2.3% of operating profit. That is well below the 10% materiality threshold, and the mechanical signal is **"Flag: immaterial"**. The Hold rating is this report's reading of that signal: an effect below the threshold gives no basis for a directional call. One caveat: the group base is an illustrative scale, not Unilever's reported consolidated figure, so the ratio is indicative only. An effect this small cannot move group earnings enough to support a directional view, however the market treats it.

**2. Consensus does not separate the effect, but that doesn't matter here.** Public aggregator and earnings-call transcript research found real consensus. MarketScreener shows 16 analysts at Outperform with a target of €61.43; Stockopedia shows Buy with a target of 5,287.14p (no analyst count given) against a 4,671.00p close on 2026-09-28. The research also found no public evidence that analysts separate the IAS 29 net monetary gain/(loss) from ordinary FX translation. On the Q1 and Q2 2026 calls, Argentina and Türkiye came up as volume and pricing stories, and currency appeared only as one aggregate headwind. In this report's own judgement (not a rule set by the model, whose output is only the mechanical signal), this condition would support a Sell-leaning call **if the effect were material**. It isn't, so the market's blind spot costs little. A caveat: paywalled broker notes could not be checked.

**3. What to watch.** Argentina drives the exposure. It carries a grade-C monetary exposure (ratio -18.3%) and a €-206m net monetary loss, which is more than the whole group figure. Türkiye partly offsets it with a grade-A exposure and a €+11m gain. The 2025 validation matters for any future re-rating. Both subsidiaries' disclosed net monetary results are losses (Argentina €-46m, Türkiye €-10m), and the model gets the sign right but not the size. If the group net monetary loss, driven by Argentina, grew towards 10% of group operating profit (the materiality threshold) while consensus still folded it into generic "FX", then on this report's own reading (as described in point 2) the call would move from Hold towards a **Sell-leaning** call. Until then, there is no informational edge to act on.
