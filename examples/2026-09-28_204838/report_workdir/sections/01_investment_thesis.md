## Investment Thesis

Unilever plc (LSE: ULVR) is a global consumer-goods group that reports under IFRS. Two of its operating subsidiaries sit in economies classified as hyperinflationary: Argentina (ARS, hyperinflationary since 1 July 2018) and Türkiye (TRY, since 1 July 2022). For both, Unilever restates the local accounts under IAS 29 into current purchasing power, then translates them at the closing rate under IAS 21. Each year it also reports how much that treatment moves the group's total assets, turnover and operating profit, along with the resulting net monetary gain/(loss), which makes it an unusually clean case study in how hyperinflation accounting flows through to group figures.

On the model's 2024 figures under the actual IFRS treatment, the modelled group has total assets of about €46,545m, revenue of about €60,845m and operating profit of about €8,496m. These totals use an illustrative rest-of-group scale, not Unilever's reported consolidated figures. Against that base, the hyperinflationary subsidiaries produce a combined net monetary loss of about €195m. Argentina's loss of about €206m more than accounts for it, and Türkiye partly offsets it with a gain of about €11m.

The model's earnings-quality read is **"Flag: immaterial"**. The net monetary loss is about **2.3% of group operating profit**, well below the model's 10% materiality threshold. Because the group base is illustrative, the ratio is indicative only. On this measure, the IAS 29 monetary effect does not move the needle at group level in 2024.

The tension this report explores is that the effect is dramatic at the subsidiary level: Argentina alone carries grade C on the model's A–F monetary-exposure scale, the weaker of the two subsidiary grades. It is less clear whether that should register as a group-level concern, or whether a roughly €195m item inside an €8.5bn operating-profit base is simply noise. The Bulls Say and Bears Say sections take up that question.

On market perception, public consensus (Stockopedia: Buy, target 5,287.14p against a 4,671.00p close on 28 September 2026; MarketScreener: 16 analysts, Outperform, target €61.43) shows no sign that it separates the IAS 29 net monetary gain/(loss) from ordinary FX translation. On earnings calls and in aggregator commentary, Argentina and Türkiye appear only as volume and pricing stories inside a generic aggregate "currency headwind". Paywalled broker notes could not be checked.
