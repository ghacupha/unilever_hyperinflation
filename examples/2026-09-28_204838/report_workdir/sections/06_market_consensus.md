## Market Consensus Comparison

Public sell-side consensus on Unilever (MarketScreener: 16 analysts, Outperform, target €61.43; Stockopedia: Buy, target 5,287.14p) sits alongside earnings calls that mention Argentina and Türkiye only as volume and pricing stories. On the Q1 2026 call, management treated them as volume stories, citing "high single digit growth in Argentina" and "strong performances in markets such as India, Turkey, and Vietnam". Currency came up only as one aggregate number: "FX reducing the reported turnover by 7.7%". On the Q2 2026 call, the one related analyst question treated hyperinflation as a Latin America pricing issue ("pricing was obviously flat in the quarter despite the hyperinflations..."). Management's answer again gave currency as one figure: a 4.9% hit to H1 turnover and roughly 6 points off underlying EPS growth.

**None of this commentary separates the IAS 29 net monetary gain/(loss) from ordinary FX translation.** Both are folded into a generic "currency headwind". Unilever's own USG methodology also caps hyperinflationary price growth, which keeps the monetary line out of the headline growth metric. One caveat: paywalled broker notes (Deutsche Bank, Barclays, Bernstein) were not accessible.

At group level, this silence is roughly proportionate. The model's 2024 net monetary loss of €195m is about 2.3% of the €8,496m modelled operating profit, well below the 10% materiality threshold (the group base is an illustrative scale, not Unilever's reported figure, so the ratio is indicative only). The aggregate framing does understate how concentrated the exposure is, though. Argentina alone books a €206m loss, grades C on monetary exposure and has an exposure ratio of -18.3%. Türkiye's €11m gain (grade A) partly offsets it. Aggregate "FX" language on calls and in aggregator commentary hides that offset. The per-subsidiary split is disclosed in the Form 20-F hyperinflation note, but it is not what the market discusses, so it is easy to miss that a single subsidiary's loss is larger than the whole net effect.
