## Risks & Uncertainty

**Argentina is the weak link.** Argentina carries a monetary-exposure grade of C on the model's A–F scale, the weaker of the two subsidiary grades. In 2024 its net monetary loss was €206m, about 18% of its €1,125m total assets. The group ratio is small: net monetary loss of €195m against €8,496m operating profit, or 2.3%, below the 10% materiality threshold. Argentina alone more than accounts for it. Türkiye (grade A) booked a small €11m gain, about 2.6% of assets. A further Argentine inflation or FX shock hits this line directly.

**The model is illustrative, not a reconstruction.** The subsidiary inputs are fictional figures solved to reproduce Unilever's real 2024 disclosure (matched almost exactly). The 2025 out-of-sample test matched sign on only 3 of 4 lines per subsidiary; magnitudes missed: Türkiye's modelled net monetary loss was €113m against €10m disclosed, and Argentina's operating profit was −€1.4m against −€54m. The total-assets line cannot turn negative under this model's mechanics, yet real 2025 was −€199m for Argentina. Forward projections deserve far less confidence than the 2024 calibration. The 2.3% materiality ratio uses an illustrative group scale, not Unilever's reported consolidated figures.

**The macro regime has already changed.** 2024's positive impacts came from inflation (118% Argentina, 44% Türkiye) outpacing managed FX depreciation (~19%, ~18%). Argentina floated the peso in April 2025, and the lira kept depreciating while inflation slowed. Both plausibly reversed that relationship.

**The market doesn't price this separately.** Public transcripts and aggregators fold the net monetary line into a generic "currency" headwind; paywalled broker notes could not be checked. At today's immaterial scale that blind spot costs little, so consensus (target 5,287p vs 4,671p close) is not mispricing it. The risk is conditional: if the group net monetary loss, driven by Argentina, grew towards the 10%-of-operating-profit materiality threshold, the market's aggregate "FX" framing could leave it mispriced.

**Classification could change.** Türkiye's hyperinflationary status rests on IAS 29.3's qualitative indicators, not a bright-line test (IFRS Interpretations Committee, July 2025). Headline inflation has eased to ~31%. If Türkiye were reclassified, only its IAS 29 effect would disappear. Argentina's would remain, and losing Türkiye's €11m gain would slightly widen the group net monetary loss (from about €195m towards about €206m).
