## Economic Moat

**Moat Rating: Not assessed**

This model contains no brand, scale or competitive-position analysis, so it does not rate Unilever's group-level moat. What it can say is that the hyperinflation data does not show a group-level problem: the combined net monetary loss of about €195m is only 2.3% of modelled group operating profit (€8,496m, an illustrative group scale rather than Unilever's reported figure, so the ratio is indicative only). The subsidiary-level figures below describe accounting exposure, not competitive position, so they do not support a local moat rating either.

**The restatement says nothing about pricing power.** Under IAS 29, 2024 turnover is about €417m higher than under World A, but operating profit is only about €6m higher. Restated COGS adds about €255m, and other costs absorb nearly all of the rest. Operating margin slips from 14.05% to 13.96%. That near-one-for-one offset is built into the model: revenue, COGS, opex and depreciation are all restated by the same in-year flow factor, so it is an artefact of the restatement, not evidence about whether Unilever's prices keep pace with local inflation. The same applies to Argentina's €230m turnover uplift becoming €10m of operating profit, and to Türkiye's €187m uplift coming with a €4m operating-profit *reduction*. In the 2025 disclosures, both operating-profit impacts turned clearly negative: Argentina −€54m and Türkiye −€46m. The model's 2025 macro assumptions have FX depreciation outpacing inflation, which would plausibly explain the reversal, but that is an assumption, not a disclosed fact.

**Monetary-exposure grades differ sharply between the two.** Argentina is graded **C**. Its net monetary loss of about €206m equals **−18.3%** of its €1,125m of total assets, even though the model has it in a net monetary *liability* position. Türkiye is graded **A**, with an exposure ratio of **+2.6%** (an €11m gain on €420m of assets). The overall grade is **C**. These grades reflect the model's mechanics, not evidence about how management runs either balance sheet: the subsidiary-level monetary-asset/liability split and opening equity are illustrative modelling choices calibrated to Unilever's disclosed aggregates, not reported figures.

**Duration of exposure is not what drives the grades.** Argentina has been hyperinflationary since July 2018 and Türkiye only since July 2022. The longer-running exposure shows the larger loss. That loss (Argentina's €206m) is Unilever's actual disclosed 2024 figure, which the calibration was solved to reproduce. Only the subsidiary asset base and monetary split behind the grades are illustrative, so the grades say nothing about any measured difference in how the two subsidiaries are managed.

Subsidiary-level figures are illustrative calibrations to Unilever's disclosed aggregates, not reported subsidiary accounts.
