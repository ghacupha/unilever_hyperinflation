## Bulls Say
- Capital position is a genuine strength (financial_health capital grade A): total capital ratio of 19.6% sits well above the 14.5% regulatory minimum, giving Family Bank headroom as CBK's core capital minimum rises to Shs 5bn by 31 December 2026 — a bar that is expected to force consolidation among weaker, thinner-capitalized peers — and continues climbing toward Shs 10bn by 2029, while Family Bank stays comfortably compliant.
- Liquidity is equally strong (grade A): the model's liquidity ratio starts at 53.7% in 2026 and rises to 60.5% by 2030, nearly triple the 20% regulatory floor, leaving room to fund loan growth without straining the balance sheet.
- The single largest swing factor in the valuation is cost of funds (±22.7% on average PAT for a 100bp move in deposit pricing), and it is skewed favorably if policy-rate transmission continues to ease deposit costs following the 2025 CBR cuts referenced in the sector outlook.
- Underlying operating momentum was real going into the forecast: NIM expanded from 7.6% to 8.6% and cost-to-income improved from 74.8% to 69.4% in FY2025, with ROA rising from 2.1% to 2.6% — genuine operating leverage, not just balance-sheet growth — and the FY2025 capital raise (357m new shares via Restricted Equity Offer) further strengthened the base ahead of the NSE listing.
- The best-case scenario puts blended fair value at KES 18.52/share, roughly 30% above the KES 14.20 base case, driven mainly by residual-income upside (17.83 vs. 11.99 base) if loan growth and margins land at the top end of the sensitivity ranges.

## Bears Say
- The valuation gap is extreme, not marginal: the stock trades at KES 31.45, +121.5% above the blended fair value of KES 14.20, landing in the model's "Extreme" uncertainty tier — even generous scenario assumptions don't close this gap (best case blended value is still only KES 18.52).
- The blended fair value itself is built on a wide method spread (max-to-min range of 2.17x the median across the three methods, with the DDM's KES 7.72 the low end and the P/B-regression's KES 33.69 the high end — more than four times the DDM value); strip out the P/B-regression outlier and the DDM/residual-income average is closer to KES 9.9, which would make the price gap even wider.
- Asset quality is the weak link in an otherwise strong balance sheet: NPL is graded C at a 10.77% ratio, and the model's own projected glide-path down to 7.65% by 2030 assumes a benign credit cycle that has not yet been tested through this listing.
- Profitability is set to normalize downward, not up: the model's own ROE series falls from 21.1% in 2026 to 15.0% by 2030 as elevated post-capital-raise returns fade — a declining-return trajectory that argues against paying a premium for growth today.
- The shares are newly and thinly listed (NSE debut 23 Jun 2026, roughly five and a half weeks old at the reference date) with no analyst consensus yet; the peer-average P/B multiple implies a value of only ~KES 21.48 versus the actual KES 31.45 traded price, suggesting a scarcity/listing premium rather than fundamentals is propping up the quote, compounded by flagged pre-2027 election macro uncertainty.
