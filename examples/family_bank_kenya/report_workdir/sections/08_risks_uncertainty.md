## Risks & Uncertainty

The blended fair value of KES 14.20/share is sensitive to three factors tested in the model, each measured as a swing in average projected PAT. A slowdown in loan/balance-sheet growth (0.70x multiplier under the worst case vs. 1.20x best case) swings average PAT by -19.5%/+14.0%. A 100bp adverse parallel shift in asset yields (lending rates) cuts average PAT by 15.0%, symmetric with the upside from a 100bp favorable shift. The single largest lever is cost of funds: a 100bp adverse move in deposit pricing — plausible given CBK policy-rate transmission — cuts average PAT by 22.7%, with an equivalent 22.7% uplift if funding costs fall instead. None of these are remote tail scenarios; they sit within the range Family Bank has already experienced across 2023-2025.

The valuation itself carries wide method disagreement: DDM (KES 7.72), Residual Income (KES 11.99), and P/B regression (KES 33.69) span a method_spread of 2.17x, placing the call in the model's "Extreme" uncertainty tier. This is not noise — it reflects genuine disagreement about whether Family Bank should be priced on cash distributions, accounting residual profitability, or peer trading multiples, and the blended figure should be read as a central estimate inside a very wide band, not a precise target.

Macro/regulatory risk is elevated: pre-2027 election uncertainty is flagged as a live risk factor in the sector outlook, industry NPL peaked at 17.6% in mid-2025 (still ~16-17%, well above Family Bank's own 10.8% modeled 2026 level), and rising core-capital minimums (Shs 10bn by 2029) are expected to force sector consolidation, with the full Risk-Based Credit Pricing Model rollout by February 2026 an added source of yield-assumption uncertainty.

Finally, Family Bank listed on the NSE by introduction only on 2026-06-23, roughly 5.5 weeks before this report's reference date. There is no analyst consensus target or coverage yet, trading history is thin, and the stock's 52-week range (KES 18.00-50.00) points to high volatility. The traded price of KES 31.45 sits well above both the blended fair value and the peer-multiple proxy of KES 21.48, a gap plausibly driven by listing scarcity or growth optimism that this model does not attempt to quantify.
