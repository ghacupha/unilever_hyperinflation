## Valuation, Sensitivity & Scenarios

**Methodology.** We value Family Bank using three complementary approaches: a Dividend
Discount Model (DDM), a Residual Income (Excess Return) model, and a Price-to-Book/ROE
regression against NSE-listed peers. The three are blended 50% DDM / 30% RI / 20% P/B-ROE,
reflecting DDM as the primary method for a dividend-paying bank and RI as a cross-check.
No single universal weighting formula exists in the literature for combining these methods;
these weights are this model's own operationalization of a method hierarchy, with DDM
weighted highest given banks' regulatory capital constraints on cash payouts, and
book-value-anchored approaches (RI, P/B) supplementing rather than replacing it.

**The numbers.** DDM produces a fair value of KES 7.72/share, Residual Income KES 11.99/share,
and the P/B-ROE regression KES 33.69/share, blending to KES 14.20/share. The spread across
methods is extreme — the P/B-ROE estimate is more than four times the DDM estimate, a gap
far beyond the ~50% threshold that would normally flag divergence. This signals genuine
valuation uncertainty: the DDM and RI methods, anchored to cash payouts and current excess
returns on equity, are far more conservative than the peer-multiple regression, which
implicitly assumes Family Bank's returns and growth converge toward higher-multiple peers.
The blended figure should be read as a central estimate spanning a wide plausible range
rather than a tight point estimate.

**Net income sensitivity.** Three factors drive average projected PAT (base case: KES
7,466.9 million/year). Cost of funds (deposit pricing) — a macro factor tied to policy-rate
transmission — has the largest swing, moving average PAT by -22.7%/+22.7% on a ±100bp
parallel shift in deposit cost. Loan/balance-sheet growth, driven by the macro credit-demand
cycle (0.70x worst-case vs. 1.20x best-case growth multiplier), moves PAT by -19.5%/+14.0%,
with an asymmetric downside skew. Asset yield (lending rate), a bank-specific repricing
factor, moves PAT by -15.0%/+15.0% on the same ±100bp shift. Cost of funds is therefore the
single largest swing factor, underscoring the bank's sensitivity to deposit-pricing
competition and policy-rate transmission over its own lending-rate repricing ability.

**Scenario comparison.** Blended per-share value ranges from KES 8.34 in the Worst case to
KES 18.52 in the Best case, against a Base case of KES 14.20 — a Best-to-Worst spread of
KES 10.17, or roughly 122% of the Worst-case value. This wide range reflects compounding
uncertainty across all three valuation methods (DDM ranges KES 5.16-9.58, RI ranges
KES 3.98-17.83, P/B-ROE ranges KES 22.84-41.90 across the same scenarios) and should be
read as a plain statement of projection uncertainty rather than a tight confidence band
around the base case.
