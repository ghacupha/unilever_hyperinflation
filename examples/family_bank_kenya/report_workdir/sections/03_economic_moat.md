## Economic Moat

**Moat Rating: None**

Family Bank shows no discernible structural advantage over its NSE-listed peers on the data available. Profitability trails the peer set: FY2025 ROAE of 16.5% sits below the ~19.6% simple average of nine listed peers (Absa 24.7%, Equity 26.5%, KCB 22.5%, NCBA 19.7%, Co-op 19.1%, I&M 18.0%, Stanbic 18.0%, StanChart 18.0%, DTB 10.3%), and the model's own forward ROAE path declines from 21.1% (2026) to 15.0% (2030), consistent with a bank facing margin compression rather than pricing power. On cost efficiency, Family Bank's 69.4% cost-to-income ratio (improved from 74.8% in FY2024) is well above the one directly comparable disclosed peer figure — NCBA at 52.3% — indicating a scale/efficiency gap rather than a cost advantage.

Switching-cost and brand sources of moat cannot be substantiated from the sourced material: no branch/agent network size, digital-channel penetration, or payroll-relationship data is available to evaluate deposit stickiness. What can be said is that Family Bank has operated in the Kenyan market for roughly two decades (OTC-traded since 2006) before its NSE listing by introduction on 2026-06-23, and has grown its deposit base to KES 152.4bn — a reasonable franchise-growth signal, but not evidence of switching costs peers lack. Banking-license barriers to entry apply industry-wide and do not differentiate Family Bank from the nine peers above, all of which operate under the same CBK regime. Capital strength (19.6% total CAR vs. a 14.5% regulatory minimum) is solid but not shown to be superior to peers, since comparable peer capital data was not sourced. Improving NIM (7.6%→8.6%) and cost-to-income trends are encouraging but describe a bank narrowing a gap, not one holding an edge.
