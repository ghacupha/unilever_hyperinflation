## Market Consensus Comparison

No sell-side analyst consensus exists for Family Bank Limited (ticker FMLY) as of this report's 2026-08-02 reference date. The stock listed on the Nairobi Securities Exchange by introduction on 2026-06-23 at KES 18.00/share, after decades of trading over-the-counter — roughly 5.5 weeks of exchange history is too short for sell-side coverage to have developed, and a web search turned up no published target price or rating. We therefore do not claim a consensus where none exists.

As a stand-in, we use a proxy: the simple average trading P/B multiple of nine NSE-listed peer banks (Absa, Co-op Bank, DTB, Equity Group, I&M Group, KCB Group, NCBA, Stanbic Holdings, StanChart), which works out to roughly 1.11x. Applied to Family Bank's own book value per share of KES 19.31, this implies a proxy value of approximately **KES 21.48** — explicitly a peer-multiple proxy, not a real market consensus target.

This model's own blended fair value is **KES 14.20/share**, roughly **34% below** the KES 21.48 peer-multiple proxy. On this comparison, the model is more bearish than the market proxy — largely a function of the DDM and residual-income legs (KES 7.72 and KES 11.99/share respectively) pulling the blend well below the pure P/B-regression estimate (KES 33.69/share), which alone would sit above the peer proxy.

The more consequential divergence is against the actual traded price of **KES 31.45/share** (as of 2026-07-31), which implies a market P/B of ~1.63x — well above both the peer-average P/B (~1.11x) and this model's blended fair value, sitting **+121.5%** above it. The traded price also sits about 46% above the peer-multiple proxy itself. Given the stock's 52-week range of KES 18.00–50.00 since listing, this gap is consistent with a listing-related scarcity/liquidity premium and momentum/growth optimism not yet reflected in either peer trading multiples or this model's fundamentals-based blend, rather than a reconcilable mispricing. We treat the peer proxy as a conservative anchor and flag — rather than paper over — the width of the gap to the traded price.
