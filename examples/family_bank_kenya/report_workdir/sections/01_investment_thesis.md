## Investment Thesis

Family Bank Limited is a Kenyan commercial bank that traded over-the-counter for two decades before listing on the Nairobi Securities Exchange by introduction on 2026-06-23. On a standalone Bank basis as of FY2025, the institution reports total assets of KES 208.63 billion, total equity of KES 32.11 billion, customer deposits of KES 152.44 billion, and net loans of KES 105.90 billion, with book value per share of KES 19.31. These figures position Family Bank as a mid-tier player within Kenya's banking sector, sitting alongside NSE-listed peers such as Absa, Co-operative Bank, Equity Group, KCB Group, and NCBA.

On valuation, the model's blended fair value estimate is KES 14.20 per share, combining a dividend discount model (KES 7.72), residual income (KES 11.99), and a peer P/B regression (KES 33.69) under 50%/30%/20% weights. Against the current NSE traded price of KES 31.45, this blended fair value implies the stock is trading roughly 121.5% above the model's estimate of intrinsic worth — a substantial premium that reflects, in part, the newness and thinness of the FMLY listing and the wide dispersion across the three underlying valuation methods.

That dispersion itself frames the central tension for this name: the market-multiple approach (P/B regression) suggests meaningfully more value than the income-based approaches (DDM and residual income), leaving open whether Family Bank's post-listing scarcity premium and growth trajectory justify a re-rating toward peer trading multiples, or whether the more conservative income-based methods better capture underlying cash-generation reality — a question this report's Bulls Say/Bears Say section takes up directly.

Given this gap between traded price and blended fair value, the model's mechanical signal is currently **Hold** — the Extreme-uncertainty band around this valuation means the price would need to fall well below fair value to trigger a Buy signal mechanically, and while the premium is large, it does not by itself cross the Sell threshold. Whether that mechanical read holds up is addressed separately in the Recommendation section.
