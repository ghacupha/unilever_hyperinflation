## Financial Health

**Overall Grade: C**

The overall grade is the worst of three sub-grades — capital, liquidity, and asset
quality (NPL ratio) — each assessed against a simple, rule-based test: capital and
liquidity are graded on their buffer above the CBK regulatory minimum, while the NPL
ratio is graded against fixed absolute bands. There is no weighting or model behind this;
it is a straightforward pass/fail-style check, and here the weakest link sets the
overall score.

**Capital: A.** The total capital ratio is projected at 19.6% for the nearest projected
year (2026), against a CBK minimum of 14.5% — a buffer of roughly 5 percentage points.

**Liquidity: A.** The liquidity ratio is projected at 53.7%, well above the 20% CBK
minimum — a buffer of over 30 percentage points.

**Asset quality (NPL): C.** The projected NPL ratio is 10.8% for 2026, and this is the
sub-grade pulling the overall score down to C despite comfortable capital and liquidity
buffers. That matters for the investment case: Family Bank is not capital- or
liquidity-constrained, but loan-book credit quality is the binding weakness, and a bank
trading at a discount on paper can still disappoint if provisioning has to catch up with
non-performing loans. For context, sector-wide gross NPL peaked at 17.6% in mid-2025
before moderating, so Family Bank's 10.8% sits meaningfully better than the industry
trend, even though it is not yet "clean" on an absolute basis.

This snapshot reflects the nearest projected year (2026) only, not a long-run average.
The projection shows steady improvement across the horizon — capital holds near 19.4–19.8%,
liquidity rises to over 60% by 2030, and the NPL ratio declines to 7.7% by 2030 — so the
asset-quality drag on the grade is expected to ease over the forecast period rather than
persist.
