## Investment Thesis

Unilever plc (ULVR, London Stock Exchange) is a global consumer-goods group that reports under IFRS. Two of its subsidiaries operate in economies classified as hyperinflationary. Argentina (ARS) has been hyperinflationary since 1 July 2018 and Türkiye (TRY) since 1 July 2022. For both, Unilever first restates the local accounts for inflation under IAS 29. It then translates the restated figures into euros at the closing rate under IAS 21. Because of this two-step treatment, the subsidiaries' reported results reflect both local price-level changes and currency movements.

Our model reports 2024 consolidated figures of about €46,545m in total assets, €60,845m in revenue and €8,496m in operating profit, all under the actual IFRS treatment. These group totals are an illustrative model scale, not Unilever's reported consolidated accounts. It also records a net monetary loss of €195m. Most of that loss comes from Argentina (−€206m), and Türkiye partly offsets it with a small gain (+€11m). The IAS 29 restatement also adds €230m (Argentina) and €187m (Türkiye) to reported turnover. The operating-profit effects are much smaller: +€10m and −€4m.

The model's mechanical earnings-quality signal is **"Flag: immaterial."** The net monetary loss equals about 2.3% of the model's group operating profit (an indicative ratio), well below the model's 10% materiality threshold. The model's worst-exposed subsidiary is Argentina, with a monetary-exposure grade of C, and the group's overall grade is also C.

The central tension in this thesis is that the hyperinflation mechanics are dramatic at the subsidiary level. Argentina's monetary loss alone is roughly 18% of its total assets in the model. It is an open question whether that should matter to a group-level investor when the consolidated effect rounds to a low single-digit share of operating profit.

On market perception, analyst consensus sits at a mean target price of 5,080p according to TipRanks (10 analysts, "Moderate Buy"), against a share price of 4,664.50p at the 25 September 2026 close (Trading Economics). The public commentary we reviewed does not appear to separate the IAS 29 effect from ordinary FX translation. Argentina and Türkiye come up as volume-growth stories plus a generic "currency headwind." Neither the net monetary gain/(loss) line nor IAS 29 was mentioned on the 2026 earnings calls we reviewed. Paywalled broker notes were not accessible, so this conclusion covers public sources only.
