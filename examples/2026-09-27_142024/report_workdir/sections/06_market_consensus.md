## Market Consensus Comparison

Headline consensus is mildly positive. TipRanks puts ULVR at "Moderate Buy" (5 Buy / 3 Hold / 2 Sell, 10 analysts) with a mean target of 5,080p, against a 4,664.50p close on 25 September 2026. The public commentary on the hyperinflationary subsidiaries is thin, though. On the Q2 2026 call, the CEO described Argentina only in operating terms ("Argentina delivered high single-digit volume growth"). The CFO talked about currency only as one aggregate translation figure: "Currency reduced our first half turnover by 4.9%". On the Q1 call, Türkiye came up as a market with "strong performances". Press summaries of the consensus mention bears citing "foreign exchange headwinds" in general. Unilever's own framing deals with hyperinflation mainly by excluding price growth above 26% a year from underlying sales growth.

**We found no commentary that separates the IAS 29 net monetary gain/(loss) from ordinary FX translation.** No analyst asked about it, and none of the material names IAS 29. One caveat: paywalled broker notes were not accessible, so this conclusion rests on call transcripts, company filings and aggregator summaries.

The model's own figures suggest this silence is broadly proportionate at the group level. The 2024 net monetary loss of €195m is about 2.3% of the model's €8,496m operating profit (an illustrative model scale, not Unilever's reported consolidated figure, so the ratio is indicative), well under the 10% materiality threshold. The subsidiary picture is different. Argentina alone booked a €206m monetary loss, equal to an exposure ratio of −18% of its total assets (grade C), which Türkiye's €11m gain (grade A) only slightly offsets. The IAS 29 restatement also added €230m (Argentina) and €187m (Türkiye) to turnover. Generic "FX headwind" language therefore misses Argentina's IAS 29 monetary loss, which the model books even though Argentina holds a net monetary *liability* position (about ARS 407bn), because restated opening equity and operating profit grow with inflation alongside it. Consensus is not wrong on group earnings, but it does not see this line at all.
