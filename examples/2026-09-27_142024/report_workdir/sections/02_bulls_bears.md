## Bulls Say
- **The hyperinflation line looks small at group level.** The 2024 net monetary loss of €195m equals about 2.3% of the model's group operating profit (€8,496m, an illustrative model scale, not Unilever's reported consolidated figure, so the ratio is indicative), well below the model's 10% materiality threshold. Operating profit barely moves between the scenarios: €8,490m under World A (plain current-rate translation) against €8,496m under World C (actual IFRS, IAS 29 + IAS 21). The €6m gap means the restatement does not change the group's earnings level.
- **Türkiye's monetary exposure is small.** Its monetary-exposure grade is A, with an exposure ratio of about 2.6% of its €420m in total assets, and it booked a small monetary *gain* (+€11m) in 2024. Türkiye's disclosed 2025 net monetary effect was only −€10m. On the Q1 2026 call the CEO listed Turkey among "strong performances in markets such as India, Turkey, and Vietnam."
- **Argentina's monetary drag is shrinking.** Argentina's disclosed net monetary loss fell from −€206m in 2024 to −€46m in 2025. Operationally, the CEO reported "high single-digit volume growth" in Argentina in Q2 2026. On this evidence, Argentina is recovering as a business even as the accounting drag fades.
- **Currency headwinds are easing, and the stock trades below consensus.** Currency cut H1 2026 turnover by 4.9%, but that eased to 2.4% in Q2, and the CFO guides to about 3% for the full year at July spot rates. The shares closed at 4,664.50p against a TipRanks mean target of 5,080p (roughly 9% upside; 10 analysts, "Moderate Buy"). The 4 November 2026 Capital Markets Day is a near-term catalyst.
- **IFRS gives a smaller hit than the US GAAP alternative.** Under the World B US GAAP temporal method, the model's remeasurement loss is €272m. The IAS 29 net monetary loss Unilever actually reports is €195m. The accounting investors actually see is the less punitive of the two.

## Bears Say
- **Argentina carries the group's monetary loss, and its hyperinflation has lasted eight years.** Argentina has been hyperinflationary since 1 July 2018. Its monetary-exposure grade is C: a −€206m loss on €1,125m of subsidiary assets, an exposure ratio of about −18%. It accounts for more than the entire group net monetary loss, which drags the group's overall grade down to C. A modest Türkiye gain cannot offset a problem of that size.
- **Reported turnover is inflated, not earned.** IAS 29 added €417m to 2024 turnover (€60,845m under World C against €60,428m under World A) but only €6m to operating profit. Group ROA is lower under the actual treatment (18.25% against 18.45%), and so is asset turnover (1.307x against 1.313x). Headline growth therefore overstates underlying performance.
- **The 2024 tailwind reversed in 2025.** Unilever's disclosed IAS 29 impacts were negative on every line in 2025: turnover −€90m (Argentina) and −€16m (Türkiye); operating profit −€54m and −€46m, compared with +€10m and −€4m in 2024. A plausible explanation, encoded in the model's 2025 macro assumptions (FX depreciation of ~44%/~35% against inflation of ~30%/~28%), is that FX depreciation outran local inflation. The same mechanism that flattered 2024 now penalises results, and it depends on FX paths that management does not control.
- **The market isn't looking at this line.** We found no public sell-side note or earnings-call question that separates the IAS 29 net monetary gain/(loss) from ordinary FX translation. Unilever's underlying sales growth also excludes hyperinflationary price growth above 26% a year. If the monetary line swings, consensus is not positioned for it. Currency already cut underlying EPS growth by about 6 percentage points in H1 2026.
- **Model visibility is limited, and the consensus is mixed.** Our model's 2025 roll-forward gets the sign of the total-assets impact wrong for both subsidiaries (+€184m modelled against −€199m disclosed for Argentina; +€46m against −€20m for Türkiye). It also overshoots Türkiye's monetary loss (−€113m against −€10m). The balance-sheet restatement cannot be forecast reliably from public disclosures. Sell-side views are split: 5 Buy / 3 Hold / 2 Sell, with targets from 3,900p to 6,000p, and MarketBeat's ADR panel is at "Hold."
