## Financial Health

**Overall Grade: C**

The grade comes from a simple rule this model sets itself. It is not a Unilever or IFRS metric. Each hyperinflationary subsidiary's net monetary gain/(loss) is divided by that subsidiary's own total assets (IFRS-actual basis, EURm), and the absolute value of the ratio is banded using thresholds set in the model's own grading code: A ≤5%, B ≤15%, C ≤30%, D ≤50%, F above 50%. The overall grade is the worse of the two sub-grades.

| Subsidiary | Net monetary gain/(loss), €m | Total assets, €m | Exposure ratio | Grade |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Argentina | (206.0) | 1,124.9 | −18.3% | **C** |
| Türkiye | 11.0 | 419.8 | +2.6% | **A** |

Argentina is the whole reason the overall grade is C. Its net monetary loss equals more than 18% of its total assets. Notably, the model's solved net monetary position for Argentina is a net monetary *liability* (about ARS 407bn), which the simple textbook rule would associate with a purchasing-power gain. The loss arises because the monetary gain/(loss) nets against the inflation-driven growth of restated opening equity and operating profit, not because Argentina holds excess monetary assets. Because the monetary-asset/liability split is a modelling choice rather than a disclosure, the grade should not be read as a verdict on Unilever's actual balance-sheet management. Türkiye's position is small by comparison and close to balanced, and it produced a modest gain. At group level the combined €(195)m loss is about 2.3% of the model's operating profit (€8,496m, an illustrative model scale, not Unilever's reported consolidated figure, so the ratio is indicative). So on the model's scale the problem looks contained, but it is concentrated in Argentina.

These grades use the **primary calibrated year, 2024**. That is the year the model's local-currency inputs were solved to reproduce Unilever's disclosed IAS 29 figures. The subsidiary-level splits are an illustration built to match those disclosed totals, not figures Unilever reported. The rolled-forward 2025 year is only a directional check and does not describe the current balance sheet.
