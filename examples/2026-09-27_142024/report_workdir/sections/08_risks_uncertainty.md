## Risks & Uncertainty

**Argentina is the weak link.** Argentina carries a monetary-exposure grade of C, and that drives the group's overall C. Its 2024 net monetary loss of €206m equals about 18.3% of the subsidiary's modelled total assets of roughly €1,125m. Türkiye is graded A: its €11m gain is about 2.6% of €420m. At group level the €195m net monetary loss is only about 2.3% of the model's operating profit (an illustrative model scale, not Unilever's reported consolidated figure, so the ratio is indicative), below the 10% materiality threshold. But a further Argentine inflation or FX shock hits that one line directly, and the model shows the sign can flip. Argentina has a net monetary *liability* position yet still books a loss once equity and profit are restated alongside it.

**The model's limits.** This is a calibrated, illustrative model. It does not reproduce Unilever's actual subsidiary accounts. It matches the 2024 disclosure almost exactly because it was solved to do so. The out-of-sample 2025 roll-forward matched the sign on only 3 of 4 lines for each subsidiary, and the magnitudes were off:
- Argentina's operating-profit impact came out at −€1.4m against −€54m disclosed.
- Türkiye's net monetary line came out at −€113m against −€10m disclosed.
- The total-assets line cannot turn negative under this model's mechanics. The model shows +€184m for Argentina against −€199m disclosed.

Any forward projection deserves much less confidence than the 2024 calibration.

**Macro regime shifts.** 2024's mostly positive impacts came from inflation (118% in Argentina, 44% in Türkiye) running ahead of FX depreciation (about 19% and 18%). Argentina floated the peso in April 2025, and the lira kept depreciating. The model's 2025 macro assumptions (FX depreciation of ~44%/~35% against inflation of ~30%/~28%) encode a plausible reversal of that relationship. The disclosed 2025 turnover impacts turned negative, Argentina's operating-profit impact turned from +€10m to −€54m, and Türkiye's already-negative −€4m deepened to −€46m. Further regime changes could move these lines in either direction.

**The market is not tracking this line.** In the public call transcripts and aggregator summaries we reviewed, no analyst separated the IAS 29 net monetary gain/(loss) from generic "currency headwinds." Paywalled broker notes were not reviewed. At an indicative 2.3% of the model's group operating profit the effect looks immaterial, so this blind spot does not amount to mispricing today. It would become a risk only if the group monetary loss grew towards the 10% materiality threshold.
