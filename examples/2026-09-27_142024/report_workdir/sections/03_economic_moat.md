## Economic Moat

**Moat Rating: Not rated (the source data contain no competitive-position evidence)**

This section does not assess Unilever's competitive moat; it looks only at how hyperinflation bears on local pricing and monetary exposure. At group level the effect is small: the 2024 IAS 29 net monetary loss of €195m is only 2.3% of the model's €8,496m group operating profit (an illustrative model scale, not Unilever's reported consolidated figure, so the ratio is indicative), below the 10% materiality threshold. The local operations are a separate question, and the evidence there is weaker.

**Pricing power.** IAS 29 restatement adds €417m to group turnover (World C €60,845m vs. World A €60,428m) but only €6m to operating profit, because restated costs absorb almost all of the gain (COGS alone rises €255m). In Argentina, +€230m of turnover restatement yields only +€10m of operating profit. In Türkiye, +€187m of turnover turns into a €4m reduction. These ratios are not evidence of local pricing behaviour: the model restates revenue and costs by the same in-year inflation factor by assumption, so the operating-profit effect mechanically reflects each subsidiary's calibrated margin rather than whether local prices outpace local costs. The disclosed 2025 figures, when FX depreciation outpaced inflation, show operating-profit impacts of −€54m for Argentina and −€46m for Türkiye.

**Monetary exposure.** Argentina is graded **C**: a −€206m monetary loss, an exposure ratio of −18.3% on €1,125m of subsidiary total assets. That grade alone pulls the group's overall grade down to **C**. Türkiye is graded **A**: a +€11m gain, an exposure ratio of +2.6% on €420m.

**Track record.** Argentina has been hyperinflationary since July 2018, Türkiye only since July 2022. Argentina's operations are growing (high single-digit volume growth in Q2 2026), while its monetary loss remains the larger of the two. Türkiye's modelled monetary exposure is small, but its restated margin is the weakest of the two.

*Caveat:* the impact figures are Unilever's own disclosures. The subsidiary balance sheets behind the exposure ratios are a calibrated illustration, not reported accounts.
