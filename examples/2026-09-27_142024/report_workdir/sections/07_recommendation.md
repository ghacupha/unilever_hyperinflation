## Recommendation

**Recommendation: Hold** (on the narrow question of whether the market misprices Unilever's hyperinflation-accounting effect)

This is not a full intrinsic-value call and there is no target price here. The question is only whether the IAS 29 net monetary gain/(loss) from Argentina and Türkiye is a source of earnings-quality mispricing. The answer is no, and the reason is materiality rather than market efficiency.

**The effect is too small to matter at group level.** In 2024 the net monetary loss was €195m, against group operating profit of €8,496m in our model. That group total, like the model's revenue and total assets, is an illustrative model scale (almost all of it is a stylised "other group operations" block), not Unilever's reported consolidated accounts, so the ratio below is indicative. The loss is 2.3% of operating profit, well below the 10% materiality threshold, so the mechanical signal is *"Flag: immaterial"*. The wider restatement tells the same story. The IFRS-actual view (World C) gives operating profit of €8,496m, against €8,490m if Argentina and Türkiye were treated as non-hyperinflationary (World A), and ROA of 18.3% against 18.5%. Revenue restatement moves turnover by about €417m, but most of that is offset in cost lines.

**Consensus ignores it, but that doesn't create an edge.** Analyst coverage is mildly positive (TipRanks: *Moderate Buy*, 10 analysts, mean target 5,080p against a 4,664.50p close on 2026-09-25). None of the public commentary we found treats the IAS 29 monetary line separately from ordinary FX. On the Q1 and Q2 2026 calls, Argentina came up only as a volume-growth story and currency only as an aggregate translation headwind. Paywalled broker notes were not accessible. If the effect were material, an overlooked earnings-quality issue would argue for caution. Because it is immaterial, the fact that the market overlooks it is not something an investor can profit from.

**Where the risk sits.** Argentina accounts for more than all of the exposure: its net monetary loss of €206m exceeds the group's €195m and an exposure ratio of -18.3% of its total assets (grade C). Türkiye posted a small gain of €11m (grade A). Investors should watch Argentina's monetary position. If the group net monetary loss grew to about 10% of group operating profit (roughly €850m on 2024 operating profit, against €195m today), the mechanical signal would move to *"Flag: material"*. Until then, the hyperinflation line gives no reason to change position either way.
