## Investment Thesis

Unilever plc is a global consumer-goods group that reports under IFRS. Two of its operating subsidiaries sit in economies classified as hyperinflationary: Argentina (functional currency ARS, hyperinflationary since 1 July 2018) and Türkiye (TRY, since 1 July 2022). For both, Unilever first restates the local accounts under IAS 29 into current purchasing power, then translates them into euros at the closing rate under IAS 21. The group discloses the aggregate effect of this treatment every year but does not publish the underlying subsidiary statements. The model's 2024 consolidated figures are total assets of €46,545m, revenue of €60,845m and operating profit of €8,496m. These are illustrative: the rest of the group is modelled at an illustrative scale, not Unilever's reported ex-Argentina/Türkiye figures. The IAS 29 net monetary line is a loss of €195m. Argentina accounts for all of it and more (a €206m loss), and Türkiye partly offsets it with an €11m gain.

The model's earnings-quality read is **"Flag: immaterial"**. The net monetary loss equals about **2.3% of group operating profit**, well under the model's 10% materiality threshold. Because the denominator is illustrative, the ratio is indicative only. At group level, then, the hyperinflation effect on the purchasing-power line does not move the needle on the model's own measure. The Recommendation section tests whether that conclusion holds up.

The tension this report examines is between two levels of the business. In the subsidiary accounts the effect is dramatic: Argentina earns a "C" monetary-exposure grade, with a net monetary loss of roughly 18% of its €1,125m of total assets. For a €46.5bn group, though, the result is a rounding-sized item. Whether that gap makes it a genuine group-level concern or a teaching-grade curiosity is left to the Bulls Say / Bears Say section.

On market perception, consensus (TipRanks: Moderate Buy across 10 analysts, average target 5,080p against a 4,664.50p close on 25 September 2026) shows no sign of treating the IAS 29 net monetary gain/(loss) separately from ordinary FX translation. Public 2026 earnings-call transcripts and aggregator commentary cover Argentina and Türkiye only as volume and pricing stories inside a generic "currency headwind". That finding rests on public sources only, because paywalled broker notes were not accessible.
