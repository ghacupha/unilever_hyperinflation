## Bulls Say

- **At group level the purchasing-power hit is a rounding item.** The 2024 IAS 29 net monetary loss of €195m equals about 2.3% of group operating profit (€8,496m), well under the model's 10% materiality threshold. Colgate-Palmolive, which reports under US GAAP, reached the same "not material" conclusion for its own Argentina, Türkiye and Nigeria exposure through a completely different accounting regime.
- **Headline profitability barely depends on the accounting choice.** Across the model's three worlds, 2024 operating profit ranges only from €8,490m (World A, no restatement) to €8,496m (World C, IAS 29 plus IAS 21), and ROA ranges from 18.45% to 18.25%. Restatement adds €417m to revenue, but €255m of that is absorbed by higher COGS. Restated equity is also higher (€20,785m against €20,246m), so debt-to-equity is lower under IFRS (1.24x against 1.27x). Nothing in the core ratios hinges on how hyperinflation is booked.
- **The exposure sits in one subsidiary, not two.** Türkiye earns an "A" monetary-exposure grade. Its 2024 net monetary line was actually a gain of €11m, an exposure ratio of +2.6% of its €420m of total assets. Argentina, graded "C", has €1,125m of total assets, which is about 2.4% of the €46.5bn group.
- **The operations underneath the accounting are performing, and the market leans positive.** Management described Argentina as delivering high single-digit volume growth in Q2 2026. TipRanks consensus is Moderate Buy (5 Buy / 3 Hold / 2 Sell), with an average target of 5,080p against the 4,664.50p close, about 8.9% implied upside.

## Bears Say

- **Argentina's loss runs against the textbook, which makes it hard to forecast or hedge.** The model grades Argentina "C": its €206m net monetary loss is about 18% of the subsidiary's €1,125m of total assets. The calibrated subsidiary is in a net monetary *liability* position, which the standard heuristic says should produce a gain, yet it books a loss because restated equity and profit grow faster. The illustrative subsidiaries also hold no protective assets. By contrast, BBVA offset part of its Türkiye loss with a €1,202m inflation-linked-bond gain in 2023.
- **The 2024 tailwind reversed in 2025.** Unilever's disclosed 2025 operating-profit impact was −€54m for Argentina and −€46m for Türkiye, against +€10m and −€4m in 2024, and the turnover impact turned negative (−€90m and −€16m). The model's out-of-sample 2025 roll-forward gets the sign right on only 3 of 4 lines for each subsidiary. It misses the total-assets sign entirely (+€183.7m modelled against −€199m disclosed for Argentina) and badly understates the operating-profit hit (−€1.4m against −€54m). The effect is harder to predict than the 2024 calibration suggests.
- **The 2.3% comfort ratio stands on an illustrative denominator.** The rest of the group in the model is an illustrative scale, not Unilever's actual reported ex-Argentina/Türkiye figures, so the materiality ratio is indicative only. The wider currency picture is not small either: management said currency reduced H1 2026 turnover by 4.9% and underlying EPS growth by about 6 percentage points.
- **The market isn't looking at this line.** Public 2026 earnings-call transcripts and aggregator commentary never separate the IAS 29 net monetary gain/(loss) from generic "currency headwind". Paywalled broker notes could not be checked. Analysts' targets already range widely (3,900p to 6,000p, with 2 Sells), so a monetary-line surprise that nobody is tracking could land with little warning.
- **There is no clear exit.** Türkiye's headline inflation has eased toward ~31%, yet it remains classified as hyperinflationary on IAS 29's qualitative indicators (per EY's April 2026 list). The IFRS Interpretations Committee's July 2025 agenda decision reaffirmed that all of those indicators must be weighed, not just the headline rate. Unilever has carried Argentina under IAS 29 since July 2018 and Türkiye since July 2022. Peer Reckitt Benckiser cut its own exposure by divesting Argentina on 31 December 2025.
