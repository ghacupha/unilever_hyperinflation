## Market Consensus Comparison

The Street's view is mildly positive. TipRanks shows 10 analysts rating the stock a Moderate Buy (5 Buy, 3 Hold, 2 Sell), with an average 12-month target of 5,080p against a close of 4,664.50p on 25 September 2026. Coverage of the hyperinflationary subsidiaries is thin. On the Q2 2026 call, the only analyst reference came from David Hayes of Jefferies, who said that "pricing was obviously flat in the quarter despite the hyperinflation... some sort of price reset going on there." Management talked about Argentina only in terms of volume, noting that it "delivered high single-digit volume growth." Across the Q1 and Q2 2026 transcripts and the aggregator commentary we reviewed, Argentina and Türkiye come up as volume and pricing stories plus a generic currency headwind. **None of this public commentary separates the IAS 29 net monetary gain/(loss) from ordinary FX translation.** The Q2 2026 call does not mention the monetary line at all.

Measured against the size of the effect in our model, that silence is mostly proportionate. The 2024 group net monetary loss is €-195.0m, about 2.3% of operating profit (€8,496.2m) and well below the 10% materiality threshold. The group total does hide an offset, though. Argentina contributes €-206.0m and carries a C exposure grade, with a net monetary exposure equal to about -18% of its total assets. Türkiye adds a small gain of €+11.0m and grades A. Treating everything as "FX headwind" hides the fact that Argentina accounts for all of the net loss.

One caveat: paywalled broker notes (Jefferies, JP Morgan, UBS, Bernstein and others) were not accessible, so this conclusion rests only on public transcripts and aggregators.
