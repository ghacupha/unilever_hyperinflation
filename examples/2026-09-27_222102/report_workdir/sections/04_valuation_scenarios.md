## Valuation, Sensitivity & Scenarios

The model runs Unilever's Argentina and Türkiye subsidiaries through three accounting worlds. **World A** is the plain current-rate method: local-currency figures are translated at current FX with no inflation restatement. This is the "disappearing plant" baseline, where historical-cost fixed assets shrink in euro terms as the peso and lira depreciate. **World B** is the US GAAP temporal method, which remeasures non-monetary items at historical FX and monetary items at current FX and produces a *remeasurement* gain or loss. **World C** is the IFRS treatment Unilever actually reports: IAS 29 restatement to the closing price index, followed by IAS 21 translation at the closing rate. All three are accounting treatments of the same underlying economics. They are not three different valuations of the business.

For 2024, revenue is €60,428m in both World A and World B and €60,845m in World C. Operating profit is €8,490m in A and B and €8,496m in C. Total assets rise from €46,006m (A) to €46,108m (B) and €46,545m (C). The monetary line is zero in World A, a €271.8m remeasurement loss in World B, and a €195.0m net monetary loss in World C. The divergence has a clear shape. World C adds about €417m to revenue and €539m to total assets compared with World A, but only about €6m to operating profit. Restating both revenue and costs by the same inflation factor largely cancels out at the profit line, even though the gross revenue and asset figures move much more. The World C net monetary loss is about 2.3% of group operating profit, which is below the model's 10% materiality threshold, so the mechanical signal is "Flag: immaterial".

By subsidiary, IAS 29 added €474.0m to Argentina's total assets, €230.0m to its turnover and €10.0m to its operating profit, and produced a €206.0m net monetary loss. Türkiye's restatement added €65.0m to total assets and €187.0m to turnover, reduced operating profit by €4.0m, and produced an €11.0m net monetary gain. Argentina's loss runs against the textbook heuristic. Its solved net monetary position is a *liability*, which would normally suggest a purchasing-power gain. However, the monetary plug also nets against the inflation-driven growth of restated opening equity and operating profit. That fast-growing restated base turns an apparent "liability means gain" setup into a net loss. The heuristic is useful for orientation, but it breaks down once every line of a consolidated balance sheet is restated.

The 2025 figures were not re-fitted. They roll the 2024 model forward, and the model then matches the disclosed sign on three of four lines for each subsidiary:

- **Turnover:** Argentina −€31.3m against −€90m disclosed; Türkiye −€47.4m against −€16m.
- **Operating profit:** Argentina −€1.4m against −€54m; Türkiye −€3.3m against −€46m.
- **Net monetary result:** Argentina −€23.8m against −€46m; Türkiye −€113.3m against −€10m.

Magnitudes are off in places. The model undershoots both operating-profit lines and overshoots Türkiye's net monetary line. Total assets do not match in sign: the model shows +€183.7m against −€199m disclosed for Argentina and +€46.1m against −€20m for Türkiye. This is structural. Restating non-monetary assets for a rising price index can only increase them, and Worlds A and C divide by the same closing FX rate. The model therefore cannot produce a negative asset impact. Unilever's disclosed negative figure probably reflects disposals, impairments or a comparative-basis convention that the model does not capture. This remains an open limitation, not a clean match.

Peers disclose the same effect in different ways:

- **Coca-Cola FEMSA (Argentina) and BBVA (Türkiye, via Garanti BBVA)** also report IAS 29 effects. Coca-Cola FEMSA books a net monetary *gain* (Ps.154m in H1 2025) on its Argentine liabilities, the textbook result that Unilever's Argentina does not show. BBVA's 2023 Türkiye net monetary loss of €2,118m was partly offset by a €1,202m gain on inflation-linked bonds, a hedge that this model does not represent.
- **Colgate-Palmolive** reports under US GAAP and describes its highly-inflationary designations as immaterial, disclosing no dollar figure. Under a different accounting regime, it independently reaches the same qualitative conclusion this model reaches for Unilever.
- **Reckitt Benckiser** is an IFRS filer. It reports only a bundled "exchange and hyperinflation" revenue item (£394m in 2025, up from £24m in 2024), which shows that not every IFRS filer discloses at Unilever's level of detail.
