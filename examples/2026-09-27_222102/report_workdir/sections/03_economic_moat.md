## Economic Moat

**Moat Rating: None (Argentina) / None (Türkiye)** (local positions only; the global brand franchise is a separate, wider question)

Unilever's global brands and sourcing scale are not in question. At group level, the hyperinflation drag is small: the 2024 net monetary loss of €195m equals about 2.3% of €8,496m of operating profit. Inside the two hyperinflationary markets, the case for a durable local advantage is weaker, and it differs by subsidiary.

**Pricing power.** In 2024, restatement added €230m (Argentina) and €187m (Türkiye) to turnover but only €10m and −€4m to operating profit. That gap is largely mechanical: the model restates revenue and costs by the same flow factor, so the restatement mostly cancels at the profit line and is not itself evidence of margin cushion or pricing power. In 2025, when FX-regime changes plausibly reversed the inflation-versus-depreciation relationship (a reversal the model's 2025 assumptions encode as a modeling choice), the disclosed operating-profit impacts (−€54m and −€46m) were out of proportion to the turnover impacts (−€90m and −€16m). On the Q2 2026 call, a Jefferies analyst noted that pricing was flat in the region despite hyperinflation and called it "some sort of price reset". That remark covered the region, not Argentina specifically, and it did not say whether prices were lagging inflation.

**Monetary exposure (illustrative).** Argentina is graded **C**. Its €206m net monetary loss equals 18.3% of its €1,125m of total assets (exposure ratio −0.183). The cause is not trapped cash: the subsidiary is in a net monetary *liability* position, yet it still loses real value once restated equity and profit are rolled forward. Türkiye is graded **A**, with an €11m gain and an exposure ratio of +0.026 on €420m of assets. The overall grade is C. The monetary-asset/liability split behind these grades is a modeling choice, not a Unilever disclosure, so the grades describe the model's illustrative balance sheets rather than how management actually runs them.

**Experience.** Argentina has been hyperinflationary since July 2018; Türkiye only since July 2022. The newer market nonetheless shows the smaller monetary exposure in the model, though that split is a modeling choice rather than a disclosure, so it cannot show whether longer experience has turned into a local advantage in Argentina.

**Verdict.** Argentina: **None** locally. Volumes are holding up (high-single-digit growth in Q2 2026), but regional pricing was flat in Q2 2026 despite hyperinflation, which gives no evidence of local pricing power. Türkiye: **None** locally. This is a judgment, not something the data show: Unilever's disclosures cover only the IAS 29 restatement effects, not the subsidiary's underlying profitability, and the model's 2024 pre-IAS 29 operating result for Türkiye comes from the calibration, not from a disclosure. Neither is big enough to dent the group moat. The subsidiary-level figures are illustrative, calibrated to reproduce Unilever's disclosed aggregates.
